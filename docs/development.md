# E-RNG – development

macOS desktop app that turns structured invoice data into a **ZUGFeRD / Factur-X PDF** (PDF/A-3 with the
EN 16931 XML embedded) and, if wanted, an **XRechnung** (plain XML). Every file is validated before it can
be saved: a failed check blocks the export.

## How it works

```
form ─► draft ─► invoice model (en16931) ─► CII XML
                         └─► Typst ─► PDF/A-3b ─► Mustang combine ─► ZUGFeRD PDF ─► Mustang validate ─► save
```

- **Rust** owns the data: SQLite storage, the mapping from the form to the EN 16931 model, the rule checks
  (German texts for the findings) and the CII XML (`en16931` / `en16931-formats` crates).
- **Typst** renders the PDF: the compiler is linked into the app as a library (`render/compiler.rs`, the
  crates `typst`, `typst-pdf`, `typst-svg`, `typst-kit`, pinned to the version the output was verified with),
  no process per render. **Mustang** (with its bundled veraPDF and the official EN 16931 / XRechnung
  rules) attaches the XML and validates PDF/A, XMP and XML; it runs as a bundled sidecar, together with a
  minimal Java runtime. The app never hands out a file that did not pass.
  The Mustang jar is slimmed when the sidecars are made (`mustang.drop` in `scripts/sidecars.json`): FOP, Batik and
  XML Graphics (only for rendering invoices, which the app does with Typst), test libraries (AssertJ, ByteBuddy) and
  Janino are taken out, with the service registrations that named them; 59 → 44 MB. Rhino stays (veraPDF runs its
  PDF/A rules with it), so do the UBL bindings (for validating UBL invoices, e.g. an import). The validation reports of
  both jars were compared for valid and broken PDFs and XML and are the same; the golden set checks it on every
  upgrade.
- **Frontend**: Lit, Web Awesome, signals, German UI. Dark mode follows the system.
- Creating an invoice is one step (`creation.rs`): the rules are checked, the PDF and XML are made and validated, and only if all of it holds the draft gets its number, freezes its settings and layout, locks, and the files are kept in `<data>/invoices/<context>/` with a SHA-256 each (`originals.rs`, `invoices.files`). Nothing is stored when it fails, one creation runs per context at a time. "Exportieren als" (PDF or XML) copies a kept file after checking the hashes, an invoice created before files were kept gets them made again from its frozen data on its first output. The files are never deleted by the app. Created invoices are locked and keep the number, tax rate and seller details they were made with,
  corrections go through "duplicate". A blank tax rate means no VAT is charged: the lines are exempt (E) at 0 % with the reason `vat_note` (default § 19 UStG) as BT-120, and the document shows no rate, no VAT rows and no gross sum, only the reason (`Branding.vat_free`). A blank payment term (`payment_days`) leaves the document without due date and terms; the XML still gets the due date of the issue day, because EN 16931 asks for a due date or terms (BR-CO-25). A seller with only a tax number still meets BR-CO-26 (an identifier or VAT id), as before. German law asks for more than EN 16931 in one place, checked next to the rules (`invoice::recipient_address`, rule `§ 14 UStG`): the customer's address (street, post code, city) is required above 250 € gross and only a warning up to it (Kleinbetragsrechnung, § 33 UStDV); empty address parts are left out of the XML, which forbids empty elements. Until then a draft shows the pattern with the counter masked (`RE-2026-###`) under the title `Rechnungsentwurf`, in the preview and in `Exportieren als PDF` (`invoice_draft_pdf`: the preview as a plain PDF, no XML, no check). The date of service is one day (BT-72) or, with `delivery_end`, a period (BG-14, `Leistungszeitraum`). Numbers are assigned on export only, by a pattern in the settings (`numbering.rs`: date tokens like in layouts, `N` for the counter, `NNN` fills with zeros to three digits, `[text]` stays; the default `[RE-]YYYY-NNN` gives `RE-2026-001`). The pattern only formats. When the counter starts again is a setting of its own (`Settings.number_reset`, `Reset`: `never`, `fiscal_year` (the default), `quarter`, `month`; `Zähler periodisch zurücksetzen` in the settings): the counters run in periods (`Calendar::period`, from the issue date, the reset and the fiscal year start; a quarter is one of the fiscal year, a month a calendar month; the calendar quarters of a short first year are periods of their own, they write the same `GGGG` and `Q` as the fiscal ones that follow, while the short year shares its year period with the fiscal year after it, both write the same `GGGG`), a document keeps its counter and its issue date (`invoices.counter`, `issued_on`, schema 7) when it gets its number, and the next number is the highest counter of the period plus one. A pattern change never confuses the counters; numbers from before (no stored counter) are read back with the current pattern. A reset only works when the pattern tells the periods apart, or numbers would repeat: `numbering::reset_problem` formats three years of days and refuses two days of different periods with the same text (answers are kept per pattern, reset, start and year, it runs on every read of a context) (yearly reset with `NNN`, a fiscal year from April with `YYYY-NNN`; `never` accepts every pattern). It is reported as the field `number_reset` in `Settings::missing`, refused by `context_save` and shown under the pattern (`number_example`). The fiscal year is a context setting (`Settings.fiscal_year_start`, none: the calendar year; a date repeats its day and month every year, before it the calendar year applies): `GGGG`/`GG` write the year it starts in, `gggg`/`gg` the year it ends in, `Q` its quarter, and letters meant as text go in `[brackets]` (schema 7 bracketed `G`, `g` and `Q` of stored patterns, `protect_new_tokens`). To continue the numbers of another program the user sets a counter (`number_sequence_set`, a dialog in the settings under the pattern): it is a floor for the current period of the reset (`Period::key`, which names the reset) in `number_floors` (state, not a setting; one per context, kind and period, removed with its context), `next = max(floor, highest + 1)`, refused at or below the highest number given in the period and dropped when it equals highest + 1. A stored prefix of an older version becomes `[prefix]YYYY-NNN` when settings are read. The seller's legal information (`legal_info`, one free text: what the legal form requires on business letters) goes into the XML (BT-33) and, as `{legal}`, into the layouts (older layouts and exported invoices say `{footer}`, it still works); the footer text of older versions moves there when settings are read (an exported invoice that froze it keeps its footer).

An **offer** (Angebot, `Kind::Offer`) is written like an invoice and numbered on its own. It lives in the `invoices` table (`kind` column, schema 8, numbers unique per context and kind) and has the life cycle of an invoice; its pattern is `Settings.offer.number_pattern` (default `[AN-]YYYY-NNN`) and its reset `Settings.offer.number_reset`, the number floors are per kind (`number_floors.kind`), and its layout and texts follow the invoices' unless `Settings.offer` has its own. Creating one (`creation::make_offer`) builds it with the totals engine and renders the PDF with Typst: no XML, no EN 16931 rule check, no Mustang. The PDF is kept with its SHA-256 under `<data>/offers/<context>/` (`InvoiceFiles.xml` is none); `output` refuses an XML for an offer, and `regenerate`, `set_paid` and `set_files` are for invoices only. An offer has no payment lines, no due date and no date of service, and is valid for a number of days (`InvoiceDraft.validity_days`, from `offer.validity_days` when the draft is made, kept when it is duplicated; blank: no validity). The day itself, the issue date plus the days, is never stored or typed: `InvoiceDraft::valid_until` works it out when the document is shown. It shows as the info row `valid-until` (`Gültig bis`, in the built-in layout after the date) and as the placeholder `{valid_until}` in the letter texts (the default closing text uses it; a text line that names it goes when there is no date) and in the text parts of layouts. Headline, headline of a draft and label of the number are the wording of a kind (`Wording`: `Settings.invoice_wording`, `Settings.offer.wording`, read with `Settings::wording(kind)`; a blank part is the default, so an issuer can write `Offerte`); credit notes keep `Gutschrift`. The settings of a kind are read through `pattern(kind)`, `layout_for(kind)`, `intro(kind)` and `closing(kind)`, a blank draft of a kind comes from `Settings::blank_draft(kind, today)`. Offers open earlier than invoices: `Settings::missing_for_offers()` asks for the seller's name and address and a pattern that parses and a reset it fits (`offer.number_pattern`, `offer.number_reset`; a broken offer pattern or reset does not block invoices). In the UI an offer is the invoice list and editor with `kind="offer"` (`/offers`, `/offers/:id`, "Angebote" first in the menu; `/` stays the invoice list): the routes render a literal each, so Lit makes a separate element per kind, and the texts that differ live in `kind.utils.ts`. The offer list has the stages drafts, valid and expired (a created offer whose valid-until day, `ListEntry.valid_until`, lies before today), no paid badge. The editor leaves out the profile, the service date and the payment card, adds `Gültigkeit (Tage)` (a number prefilled from `offer.validity_days`, the resulting day is its hint) and exports the PDF only; an id of the other kind redirects to its own route. The settings have a page `Angebote` before `Rechnungen`, the counter row and its dialog work per kind. Offers and invoices are linked, many to many (`document_links`, schema 9; `Store::link`, `unlink`, `links`): both ends in one context, the offer and the invoice side checked by the store, links are metadata and can change on drafts and created documents alike, a deleted draft takes its links with it and archiving keeps them, a copy does not inherit them, and an invoice can name the offers it is linked to: `Settings.offer_reference` is the default of new invoices (also the ones `invoice_from_offer` makes), `InvoiceDraft.offer_reference` the choice of one invoice (a switch beside its card of linked offers), and creating the invoice freezes the numbers of its created offers in `frozen_offers` (`Store::offer_numbers`, a draft's preview reads the links as they are). The document gets an info row `offer-reference` (`Zu Angebot AN-2026-003`, several numbers joined by commas, `Branding.offers`, shown by the built-in layout, `InfoField::OfferReference` for own ones); the XML does not carry it (no EN 16931 term fits an offer: BT-14 is the seller's order confirmation, BT-13 the buyer's order, BT-12 a contract, at most one). `Store::invoice_from_offer` (`offer_to_invoice`) makes the invoice draft of a created offer in one transaction: customer, positions, buyer reference and note are copied, layout and texts are the invoice defaults of the context (`Settings::blank_draft(Kind::Invoice, …)`), dated today, linked. The editor of either kind has a card named after what it holds (`Angebot` for one linked offer, else `Angebote`; `Rechnung` / `Rechnungen` on an offer; linked documents open by their row, a remove icon each, a dialog to pick the other kind of the context, the customer's documents first, archived ones included; an unsaved document is saved as a draft first), a created offer has `Rechnung erstellen` (asks first when invoices are linked; disabled while the context lacks what invoices need), and the lists mark a linked document with a link badge (`ListEntry.linked`). A new context starts with the document defaults of the active one (`inheritedDefaults`: tax, payment, patterns, fiscal year, texts, wordings and the offer settings).

**Units** are a list per context (`units` table, schema 10, `units.rs`): a label and a UN/ECE rec. 20 code, seeded with Stk. (C62), Std. (HUR) and pauschal (LS). The settings have a page `Einheiten` in each context's group (`erng-unit-list`): add, change, drag to reorder, delete; every change is saved at once in a dialog. The code is searched, not typed (`erng-unit-code`: code, German or English name; the common codes first, an arrow-key list, a hint that opens the list in the XRepository in the system browser through the opener plugin) in `units::catalog()`, a name for every code `en16931::codes::guard::unit` accepts (2162, `src-tauri/data/unit-names.json`, read on first use and shared with the browser mock; `scripts/unit-names.ts` makes it from the XRepository lists rec20 v3 = Rec. 20 rev. 17 and rec21 v2 = Rec. 21 rev. 11, English names, plus the curated German ones that come first; a test fails when a crate update changes the set, then download again and run the script); `guard::unit` still checks what is saved and names `kwh` as `KWH`; a label is unique per context, case aside, several labels may share a code. New positions (drafts, saved positions) start with the context's default unit: `Settings.unit` holds its label (none, or a label the list no longer has: the first unit; `units.utils.ts::startUnit`), chosen with the select `Standard-Einheit` at the top of the page, saved at once (`unit_default`, `Store::choose_default_unit`), marked by the same `Standard` tag as the default rate. Renaming that unit moves the setting with it, deleting it clears it without asking for a replacement, a new context inherits it with the list. A position carries its code and its label (`Position.unit`, `unit_label`): picking a unit writes both, the XML gets only the code and the document prints the label (`Branding.unit_labels`, one per position), so renaming or deleting a unit never changes a created invoice. Older rows have no label: they are read as what the code was printed as before (`units::legacy_label`, a frozen copy of the old table that is never edited, an empty code is C62 "Stk.") and are never rewritten for it. Renaming a unit or changing its code follows silently in the drafts of that context and in the saved positions it sees, shared ones included (a shared position may then show as "nicht in der Liste" in another context, its select keeps the label); created, imported and archived documents are not touched, nor is the time a draft was saved. Deleting a unit that no draft or saved position uses asks once; one that is in use opens a dialog that lists where and asks for the unit that takes its place (same context, `Store::delete_unit`), which is rewritten in the same transaction; the last unit of a context stays. The migration (schema 9 → 10) gives every context the defaults and nothing else; a context that has units already (a database stamped back) is left alone. A new context starts with a copy of the units of the active one (`inheritedUnits`, handed to `context_save`; without it the defaults), removing a context removes its units. Positions and saved positions pick from the units of the document's context (the active one on the Positionen page); a unit that is not in the list stays visible in the select, marked, and can be switched.

**VAT rates** are a list per context as well (`rates` table, schema 11, `rates.rs`): a label and a rate in percent, seeded with `Regelsatz` 19 and `Ermäßigt` 7 (a context that charged another rate before the list gets it as `Steuersatz`). The settings have a page `Steuersätze` right before `Einheiten` (`erng-rate-list`): add, change, drag to reorder, delete, every change saved at once; a rate is more than 0 and at most 100, comma or dot, stored without trailing zeros (`rates::canonical`), label and rate are unique per context, there are no VAT categories or exemption reasons in the list. `Settings.tax_rate` (blank: no VAT, see above) stays the context's default and is one entry of the list: a select `Standard-Steuersatz` at the top of the page `Steuersätze` (the rates plus `Keine Umsatzsteuer`, it applies to offers too) chooses it, saved at once like the list (`rate_default`, `Store::choose_default_rate`), and the row of the default carries a `Standard` tag. A position carries its rate and the label it was picked with (`Position.tax_rate`, `tax_label`, like `unit` and `unit_label`), none: the default; `draft::build_invoice` takes each line's rate from its position or else the default, a bad one is an input problem on `positions[i].tax_rate`, and a context without VAT ignores all of them (every line exempt). Invoice and offer positions and saved positions have a compact `USt.` select next to the unit (the rates of the list only, a position without one shows the default selected), hidden while the context charges no VAT; the page `Steuersätze` then says so. Changing a rate's value or label reaches drafts and saved positions that picked it, and the default when it had that value; deleting one that is in use (the default counts) needs the rate that takes its place; created, imported and archived documents are never touched (`Store::save_rate`, `delete_rate`, `rate_usage`). A new context starts with a copy of the active one's rates, like the units (the default comes along with the other defaults). With several rates the totals get one VAT row per rate, the highest first, and name its net amount (`Umsatzsteuer 7% auf 42,50 €`, § 14 Abs. 4 Nr. 7, 8 UStG), with one rate the row is just `Umsatzsteuer 19%` (`render/view.rs::total_rows`).


A **context** is one issuer (`context.rs`: a company today, maybe a Verein or a private person later): its `Settings` (seller, numbering, defaults) plus a brand color, stored in `contexts` (schema 5; the one settings row of older databases became context 1). An invoice belongs to the context it was made under for good (`invoices.context_id`), numbers are unique per context, and a draft keeps its context when saved again. Customers, saved positions and layouts are shared (`context_id` NULL) unless scoped to one context; the pickers get shared plus the context's own. `Settings::missing()` (and `missing_for_xrechnung()`) say what an issuer still lacks before invoices can be issued, the same things the rule check refuses later; the UI guard (`guard()` in `root.routes.ts`) keeps invoices, customers and templates closed until the active context has none missing, and the offers until it has none missing for offers (`missing_for_offers`). A context with invoices is archived, never removed; one without can be removed, and what is scoped to it is shared, moved or removed with it (`Disposition`). Exported invoices can be archived (`archived_at`), they leave the list and still count for numbers; the status menu (checkboxes: drafts, open, paid, plus archived ones) shows them on request and `Wiederherstellen` puts one back (`invoice_unarchive`). Buttons follow one order: what leaves a view or dialog (Abbrechen, Schließen) is a single button on the left, never in a button group; actions and the main action are on the right (a dialog uses `.dialog-footer` from the base styles, a page its `actions` and `primary` slots, the invoice footer has the leaving button on the left and the actions on the right). Something that is being made and not stored yet (a new invoice or layout) shows as an italic `placeholder` entry at the top of its list instead of a label in the form, and as a `Neu` tag where its status would be (the invoice footer). Leaving a view with unsaved edits is the user's call (`leave.utils.ts`: a view registers a check, the route `enter` hooks in `root.routes.ts` and the context switch ask `confirmLeave`; staying puts the address back; the settings register one, an invoice draft saves itself instead: a stored draft half a second after the last edit, a new one when it is left; quitting right while typing can lose the last half second, macOS ends the app without asking the window). Which context is active is device state (`localStorage`, `context.utils.ts`), not data; its brand is applied as `wa-brand-<name>` on `<html>`, which re-derives every brand token of the UI library.

### What the validators taught us

- Typst writes PDF/A-3b only through its export option (`PdfStandard::A_3b` in `render/compiler.rs`, the CLI's `--pdf-standard a-3b`; no `#set pdf(standard: ...)`), cannot write custom XMP and embeds only serif and math fonts of its own; its `pdf.attach` writes no Factur-X XMP and is not used. Mustang's `combine` attaches the XML and writes the Factur-X XMP (it rewrites the conformance B to U, A-3u; a strict `3b` checker complains); on a file that has Typst's own attachment the XML would be in it twice.
- Only the combined file is validated, never the Typst output. A PDF/A validator alone (veraPDF) passes files that are no valid ZUGFeRD; Mustang is the authority, the app's own rule check the first gate. No validator checks `/AFRelationship`, only `combine` sets it, so nothing may touch the PDF after `combine`. A run is valid only when Mustang's overall status is valid and its exit code is 0 (255 is invalid or a missing file); a validation takes about 1.5 to 2 s.
- Mustang prints XRechnung and Peppol rules as notices for plain EN 16931 files (BR-DE-15, BR-DE-21, R001): not errors, the gate blocks on errors only.
- A line's net amount (BT-131) is taken as given, the `en16931` crate does not recompute it: `invoice::line_from_unit_price` keeps the unit price and rounds quantity times price half away from zero to cents (`InvoiceLine::new` would derive the unit price from the net amount, 3 × 12.50 shows up as `4,1666…`). A delivery date or period is always set (§ 14 UStG, and Peppol R008 warns about the empty CII element). A reverse-charge invoice needs its statement on the page: the model may carry only the exemption code, so the view falls back to a German text per VAT category.
- XRechnung asks for more than EN 16931, which is why `Settings::missing_for_xrechnung()` exists: all four address parts of the seller (post code and city BR-DE-3/4, an empty street only Mustang refuses, PEPPOL-EN16931-R008) and contact name, phone and e-mail (BR-DE-5/6/7, PEPPOL-EN16931-R020). BIC and account holder are optional. A seller with a tax number only is identified by it as BT-29 (BR-CO-26).
- The golden set (`cargo test -- --include-ignored`) and its negative cases (no Factur-X XMP, a tampered grand total) are what notices a Mustang or `en16931` upgrade that changes behaviour. The CII files were also accepted by the KoSIT validator with the XRechnung configuration (`GOLDEN_OUT=<dir>`, then `-s scenarios.xml`). Mustang 2.26.0 ships veraPDF 1.30.2 (advisory GHSA-5gvp-6g43-g47v, fixed in 1.30.3); the sidecars are about 95 MB (Mustang 44, JRE 51); Typst, linked in, adds about 44 MB to the binary. The release profile builds one codegen unit and strips symbols (78 to 51 MB, about 3 minutes from scratch); LTO was measured and left out: thin gives 59 MB, fat 49 MB for twice the build time, and packed for the update all of them are 16 to 17 MB.

## Requirements

- macOS on Apple silicon (the pinned sidecars are arm64 only)
- [Bun](https://bun.sh) (version in `.bun-version`), Rust stable, Node (version in `.node-version`)
- No Java needed: the build links its own runtime

## Develop

```sh
bun install
bun run generate     # app icons (from the Icon Composer export src/assets/icon-default.png) and sidecars: downloads Mustang
                     # and a JDK (checksum-pinned), links the JRE
bun run dev          # tauri dev
```

`bunx vite dev --config .configs/vite.config.ts` runs the UI in a plain browser against a small mock
backend (`src/common/utils/mock.utils.ts`): good for looking at layouts, not for rules or totals.

| Script                           | Does                                                                     |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `bun run check`                  | typecheck (`tsc`)                                                        |
| `bun run lint`                   | rustfmt, clippy (`-D warnings`), eslint                                  |
| `bun run format`                 | rustfmt and eslint `--fix` (also runs on commit)                         |
| `bun run test`                   | vitest and `cargo test`                                                  |
| `bun run generate:sidecars:stub` | empty sidecar placeholders, enough for lint and typecheck (what CI does) |
| `bun run build`                  | `tauri build`                                                            |

Tests that run the real tools (Java, Mustang: golden invoices, negative cases) are ignored by
default:

```sh
bun run generate:sidecars
cd src-tauri && cargo test -- --include-ignored
RENDER_OUT=/some/dir cargo test every_golden -- --include-ignored   # keeps the PDFs to look at
GOLDEN_OUT=/some/dir cargo test                                      # writes the CII XML of the golden set
```

## Layout

```

  views/                   invoices and offers (list + detail), invoice-edit, customers, templates, settings (+ root shell and routes)
  common/                  components (one folder each: `<name>/<name>.component.{ts,css}`, styles always in the css file, `ErngElement.withStyles(styles)`), layouts (split, cards, editor: slots only, split and editor have a `wa-split-panel` the user can move (their slots are the panels, so they style their own slots; the divider position is stored under the `position-key` of the view; the list has a minimum and maximum, the detail a minimum; the editor shows only the form where the minimums of both sides do not fit, a container query), views import no styles), api.utils.ts (typed commands), format and form helpers, `reorder.utils.ts` (the drag and arrow-key reorder of the positions, the units, the VAT rates and the parts of a layout: `Reorder` renders the handle, its styles are `reorder.utils.css`), mock backend
src-tauri/src/
  backup.rs                backup, restore and remove all data (`.erng-backup`)
  invoice/                 profile choice, rule checks, German finding texts, CII writer, golden test set
  draft.rs                 form draft → invoice model
  units.rs                 units of measure: catalog (data/unit-names.json), defaults, the old labels, checks
  rates.rs                 VAT rates: defaults, how a rate is read and checked, where one is used
  store.rs, model.rs       SQLite and the stored shapes
  render/                  invoice layouts (Typst, compiled in `compiler.rs`) and the presentation view of an invoice
  layout_files.rs          own layouts: fonts (family from the file) and pictures (size, pixels)
  layout_bundle.rs         a layout as a file (`.erng-layout`): export, import with its fonts and pictures
  package.rs               Mustang: combine and validate, report parsing
  pipeline.rs             produce and validate the PDF and XML in a work directory
  creation.rs, originals.rs  create an invoice or offer (check, number, freeze, lock, keep the files) and hand the kept originals out
  sidecar.rs               running the bundled tools (Mustang, Java) with a deadline
  commands/                one tauri command per file
scripts/                   sidecars.ts and its pins (sidecars.json), icons.ts
src-tauri/test-fixtures/   a font file for the layout tests (Open Sans, OFL)
```

## Data

The database and the layout files live in the app data directory (`~/Library/Application Support/dev.enke.e-rng`). A
debug build (`tauri dev`) uses `dev.enke.e-rng.dev` next to it, so a development run never migrates the data of an
installed app: an older version refuses a database a newer one has migrated ("written by a newer version"). To start a debug build empty (a fresh setup, someone else's case) without moving that folder away, point it elsewhere: `ERNG_DATA_DIR=/some/dir bun run dev`.

## Backup

German invoices must stay available for years (Buchungsbelege 8, Bücher 10 years), so everything can be backed up
and restored: settings page `Datensicherung` (app-wide, not per context; `erng-backup`), code in `backup.rs`,
commands in `commands/backup.command.rs`.

**Format** (`.erng-backup`): a ZIP (deflate) with

- `e-rng.sqlite3`: a consistent snapshot of the live database made with `VACUUM INTO` (one transaction, the
  write-ahead log included; the files are never copied under WAL),
- every file under `invoices/`, `offers/` and `layouts/` with the same relative path as in the data folder,
- `manifest.json`: `format` (`e-rng-backup`), `version` (the layout of the file, 1), `app_version`, `schema_version`
  (`PRAGMA user_version` of the snapshot), `created_at` (UTC) and `files`, each with `path`, `size` and `sha256`.

The snapshot is taken before the files are listed, so every file a row knows is in the archive. The archive is
written to a temp file next to its target and renamed when complete. The time of the last backup is
`app_state.last_backup_at` (written into the snapshot as well, so restored data knows when it was backed up); the
table is created on every open (`ensure_app_state`) because it came with schema 11 before that was released.

**Restore** checks everything before it touches anything, in a staging folder inside the data folder
(`.erng-restore-*`): format and version (a newer `version` is refused), the names (only `e-rng.sqlite3` and files
below the three folders, no way out of the folder), the manifest against the archive (no file more or less), size
and SHA-256 of every file, then the database: it opens, `PRAGMA integrity_check` is `ok`, and it migrates (it is
opened with `Store::open` in the staging folder, so a database that cannot be brought up to date is refused here).
Only then the data in use is saved as a safety backup and swapped: the database file and the three folders move to
`.erng-replaced-<time>`, the staged ones move in by rename, all under the lock of the `Store`, whose connection is
closed and reopened (`Store::replace_files`; commands wait meanwhile, a stand-in connection that has no tables holds
the place). When a rename fails the ones before are taken back, when the new database does not open the old files
are put back; the old data stays. After the swap the kept originals are checked against the hashes in the database
(`originals::problems`) and what is wrong is reported, not undone. The window reloads afterwards.

- **Schema**: a backup with a **newer** schema than the app (`SCHEMA_VERSION`) is refused ("aus einer neueren Version
  von e-rng"); an **older** one is migrated by the normal migration path. **Migrations of released schemas must
  therefore never be dropped or squashed:** old backups depend on them. (Steps that were never released may still be
  edited in place.)
- **Safety backups** go to `<data>/backups/`: `<time>-vor-wiederherstellung.erng-backup` before a restore,
  `<time>-vor-entfernen.erng-backup` before "Alle Daten entfernen"; the newest 3 of each kind stay. They do not count
  as the last backup. The folder is outside of what a restore replaces.
- **Alle Daten entfernen** makes the safety backup, then swaps in an empty database and no files (a first start). The
  dialog asks to type `ENTFERNEN`, the command checks the word again.
- A run that was cut off leaves `.erng-restore-*` and `.erng-backup-*` folders, which are cleared at the next start;
  `.erng-replaced-*` stays, it may hold the only copy of the old data: the page `Datensicherung` warns about each
  such folder (`backup::interrupted`), links it to the Finder and deletes it on request (`backup_discard_interrupted`,
  which deletes nothing else).
- A restore or removal while an invoice is being created is refused (`Creating::any`).

## Messages

`notify(message, variant)` and `notifyError(error)` (`src/common/utils/notify.utils.ts`) show a toast bottom left: the
answer to something the user just did (saved, failed, already there). What stays true while they work, a locked
invoice, what is still missing, an empty list, the failure of an export inside its dialog, is a `wa-callout` on the
page.

## Icons

`<wa-icon name="save">` resolves against the library registered in `src/common/utils/icon.utils.ts`: a few Font Awesome
Free SVGs shipped with the app (the Web Awesome default library would load them from a CDN, which an offline app
cannot). Names say what an icon means (`save`, `trash`), add one by importing its svg there. Buttons with a label have no
icon; only the actions inside a list (remove a position, save it as a template) are icon only, with a `title` and an
`aria-label`.

## Layouts

A layout is a **frame** and **parts**. The frame (`PageSetup.margins`) is where the flowing content (title, intro, the
positions, totals, notices, payment, closing: `render/layouts/standard.typ`; the positions have a column `USt.` with the rate of each line
unless no VAT is charged) stays; the parts are placed around it by
an anchor (one of the nine points of the page) and an offset in millimetres towards the inside: information block
(`infos`), the address, an image, a text (bold and italic as markdown, `{placeholders}`, a font family),
the page number, a rule. Text, rule, page number, infos and address can have a color (`color`, `#rrggbb`, none keeps the grey or black of the kind; on infos and address the labels and the sender line stay grey; pictures have none): it is checked where a layout comes in (`CustomLayout::invalid_color`, in `layout_save` and when a layout file is read), `prelude.typ` sets it as the text fill or the stroke of the rule. Every page has such a setup; the first page can have one of its own (`first`) with the same
tools. The built-in layout, _Klassisch_, is such a list (`layout::builtin()`, a window letter after DIN 5008 form B), a new
layout starts as a copy of it, so there is no logo setting: a logo is an image part.

Text parts take placeholders (`render/parts.rs::placeholder`; the part form has a menu `Platzhalter einfügen` that puts one at the cursor). Single fields give the plain value, empty when unset: `{seller_name}`, `{seller_street}` (address lines 1 to 3, joined by line breaks), `{seller_postcode_city}`, `{seller_phone}`, `{seller_mobile}`, `{seller_fax}`, `{seller_email}`, `{seller_website}`, `{seller_contact_name}`, `{seller_tax_number}`, `{seller_vat_id}`, `{bank_name}`, `{iban}` (grouped when the layout's `iban_groups` is on, the first credit transfer), `{bic}`, `{account_holder}`. Blocks of lines: `{seller_address}`, `{seller_contact}`, `{seller_registration}`, `{legal}` (older layouts: `{footer}`), `{bank}`, and `{valid_until}` (offers). A line with at least one placeholder whose placeholders all come out empty goes, the fixed text on it too (`St.-Nr.: {seller_tax_number}` is gone without a tax number), so an unset field leaves neither a gap nor a label; a line without a placeholder stays, even when it is empty. A label in a part of its own cannot know that the value in the next part went, so labels and values that belong together go in one part: a tab in a line (the button `Spalte einfügen`, a typed or pasted tab does the same; not the Tab key, which keeps moving the focus) splits it into columns, `Telefon:⇥{seller_phone}` over `Mobil:⇥{seller_mobile}` is one grid (`render/parts.rs::runs` cuts the columns before the placeholders are filled, so a tab in a value stays a character; the template draws a part with a tab as a Typst grid of auto-width columns, 1.5 mm apart, its rows as far apart as the lines of a part without a tab (`par.leading`), all lines share it so the columns line up; a line with fewer columns lets its last one take the rest, a value of several lines goes under its first line in its column). Bold and italic work in every column and go on over a tab. A row whose values are all empty is gone from the grid like any other line. Mobile, fax, website and the name of the bank are seller fields for layouts only (`Seller.mobile`, `fax`, `website`, `bank_name`, all optional; `Branding.seller`, `InvoiceView.seller_fields`): they are not in the XML. The built-in layout shows them: the contact block (`{seller_contact}`) gets `Mobil …`, `Fax …` and the website (as it is) between the phone and the e-mail, the bank block (`{bank}`) a `Bank` line and the payment lines `Bank …` in front of the account holder. A payment line of several items (`Bank … · Kontoinhaber … · IBAN … · BIC …`) breaks between the items and keeps each dot with the item before it (`payment-line` in `standard.typ`); one with an item of 70 characters or more breaks wherever it must. The contact has up to six lines now, so the footer of the built-in layout starts higher (rule at 257 mm, columns at 260 mm, bottom margin 42 mm) to keep the columns clear of the legal text and everything in the area a desktop printer prints.

The page number part can stay away from a document of a single page (`several_pages_only`, off by default and for layouts made before; the switch `Nur bei mehreren Seiten` in the part form; the template tests `counter(page).final()`); the built-in layout asks for it.

Rust builds `PageView` for the template (`render/parts.rs`: text cut into runs, placeholders filled, images copied next to
the template and measured, `data.json`), `prelude.typ` places the parts in the `background` of every page. Typst has one
margin per page style, so when the first page has another frame the content is laid out in two runs (`two-runs`): units
(head, table rows, totals, …) are measured and as many as fit go to the first page, the rest follows on pages with the
other frame.

The order of the list is the order of drawing: later parts lie over earlier ones (`render/parts.rs` sorts only the pictures to the front, stably, and keeps each part's list index for the sample's boxes), the editor lists them in that order and moves a part by its handle (`erng-page-setup-form`: drag or arrow keys; the rows are keyed by their part, so what is open stays with it, and the hovered and open indices and the sample's boxes follow the move until the next rendering). There is no separate stationery: a letterhead is a picture part as wide as the page (210 mm, at 0 and 0), one per page setup, and pictures always lie behind the other parts. Fonts are a list of uploaded files, one per face: a collection (`ttc`, `otc`) is split into a standalone file for each face when it comes in (`layout_files::store_font`, also for the fonts of a layout file), a single file is kept as it is. The family is the one Typst files the font under (`layout_files::family_name`, after `FontInfo::from_ttf` of the bundled Typst): the first record of name id 1 in table order whatever its language, Mac Roman records included (macOS's own fonts have only those), minus the style words at its end (`Open Sans Light` is `Open Sans`); the typographic family (16) is not used, and neither are Typst's few per-font exceptions. A family stored differently would not be found by the layout. The style is only a label, English records before other languages and the typographic subfamily (17) before the plain one (2). Images are PNG, JPG
or SVG up to 5 MB; a raster image with fewer than 150 dpi at its placed size is flagged, the layout sample marks it with a
warning triangle (`Branding.marks`, never in a real invoice).

How numbers, money and dates are written is the layout's `Format` (`render/format.rs`: decimal and thousands separators, currency as symbol or code, before or after, with a space or not, `19 %` or `19%`, the minus sign, IBAN in groups, dates by a dayjs-like pattern with German month names). The default is the German way, `12,50 €` with a space, but `19%` without one, like the app itself writes it: the app's screens put no space between a number and its unit (`19%`, `210mm`, `5MB`), only amounts keep theirs (`12,50 €`).

A layout is exported to one JSON file (`layout_bundle.rs`, the form's state, not a stored one) with fonts and pictures as base64 under made-up names, so no path of the user leaks. Fonts go along only if the user says so (they have licences); without them the importer is told which families are missing. Every stored layout has a `uid` (made when it is first stored, kept through renaming, export and import) and an `updated_at` (UTC, set on each save). An import (`layout_import`, `plan` in `layouts.command.rs`) reads the file first and copies nothing until the questions are answered: the same `uid` as a stored layout is the same layout, and replacing it needs a yes (the dialog shows both times and what uses it; the row and its `id` stay, so the default and drafts keep pointing at it); a name another layout has (any case, archived too) is never taken, the user names the import instead (suggested: `Name (Import <date>)`, which does not look like a successor); anything else is stored right away, so it takes an explicit delete to remove it. The file is untrusted, fonts and pictures pass the same intake as picked ones (`store_font`, `store_image`), and a damaged or foreign file is refused as a whole. The built-in layout can be exported too, as a starting point.

`.erng-layout` is a file association of the app (`bundle.fileAssociations`, with an exported type `dev.enke.e-rng.layout` so Finder knows its kind). macOS hands a double-clicked file to the running app, or to a starting one, as `RunEvent::Opened`; `opened-files.command.rs` keeps the paths until the window asks (`opened_files`, also on the `layout-file-opened` event), the root view asks the user to confirm (`opened.utils.ts`), goes to the settings, and the settings import it like a picked file, with the same replace and name questions. It only works in a built app, not in `tauri dev`; Windows and Linux (the path comes as an argument) are not wired.

The sample hands out the box of every part (`<part-box>` metadata, queried from the compiled document, `render_sample`), so the editor can tell what is under the pointer: pointing at a part in the form or in the sample marks it blue on both sides, the open part is orange, and a click in the sample opens it in the form, or closes it when it is the open one. A curve in the same colours joins the row in the form and the box in the sample (`erng-editor` `links`, the ends come from the slotted elements' `anchor(key)`, here `erng-layout-form` and `erng-sheet`): one SVG over both panes in client coordinates, as tall as the pane and sticky in it on a layer of its own (painted into the scrolling content, WebKit left pieces of old curves), drawn again once per frame when a pane around either end scrolls, the window or the divider moves, or something in the form changes, and on every frame while something in it opens or closes; an end that is hidden or on another page gets no curve, a partly seen one is met in the middle of what is seen, one scrolled away at its edge nearest the view, so the curve leaves the panes there and moves with the scroll. With the guides on, the texts a layout prints from its own (text parts and page numbers, not the data of the invoice) also show what cannot be seen (`render/invisible.rs`, codes from `invisible.rs`): a pilcrow at the end of every line, an arrow where a tab ended a column, a dot on every normal space (all in violet, hanging in the text so widths and line breaks stay), and a violet badge with a short code in front of each character a paste brings along that cannot be seen or behaves unlike it looks: `NBSP`, `NNBSP`, the other spaces of U+2000 to U+200A, U+205F and U+3000 (`NQSP` … `HSP`, `MMSP`, `IDSP`), `MVS`, `ZWSP`, `ZWNJ`, `ZWJ`, `WJ`, `CGJ`, `BOM`, `OBJ` (an attachment left behind by Notes or Pages), `SHY`, `NBHY`, `LS`, `PS`, `LRM`, `RLM`, `ALM`, the bidi controls (`LRE` … `PDI`) and control characters by their names (`NUL`, `CR`, `DEL`, `NEL`, `C1`). The characters stay in the text, only the badge adds its width, so shaping, breaking and direction are those of the document. The marks are runs of the text with a `mark` (`Run.mark`, `PartViewContent::PageNumber::marked`) that `stage` makes only for `Branding.guides`, `render_pdf` switches guides off whatever the branding says, so a document and the preview without guides carry none (`marks_of_invisible_characters_are_made_for_the_guides_only`). The text part form lists such characters under the field (`invisible.utils.ts`) with a button that turns the spaces into normal ones, the non-breaking hyphen into a normal one, line separators into line breaks and drops the rest. Both read one table, `src-tauri/data/invisible-chars.json` (code point, code, replacement); control characters are known by rule. Seller and customers are cleaned the same way when they are saved (`Seller::cleaned`, `Customer::cleaned`, `invisible::clean`), silently, as they go into the XML as well: every field becomes one line (line breaks and tabs turn into spaces), only the legal information of the seller keeps its lines. Visible look-alikes such as the en dash, em dash or minus sign are typography and get no badge. The settings show the same 30-position sample in every layout (`layout_preview`), with optional guides: red for the
frame, cyan for the aids of a letter (address window, fold and punch marks, middle) and dotted outlines of the parts
(`Branding.guides`). The default layout is a setting; a layout is deleted only while no invoice uses it and it is not the
default, else it is archived. An exported invoice freezes its layout like the settings.
`cargo test -- --include-ignored` validates every golden case in the built-in layout and with letterhead pictures, a font and
image parts.
`end_to_end.rs` (ignored, needs the sidecars) takes a layout with tab columns through the code the commands use: saved, the
sample with the guides off and on, the preview and the PDF of a draft, created as an invoice (Typst, combine, validate),
exported to a layout file, imported into another store and shown again; `ERNG_E2E_OUT` names the folder that keeps its SVG,
PNG and PDF files.

What is remembered between runs is a convenience of the machine, not a setting: the dividers, the guides toggle and the last open view (`memory.utils.ts`, `localStorage`, wrapped so a missing storage breaks nothing; a blank invoice is not restored) and the size, place and state of the window (`tauri-plugin-window-state`). A divider is stored as the `position` (percent) of its `wa-split-panel`; the limits are its native `--min`/`--max` in the layout's css, which clamp what is drawn, not the stored value.

## Open work

Not built yet, nothing here is promised. Bigger features have a plan in `.claude/plans/` (build targets, historic import, Steuerberater export, VAT categories for invoicing abroad, sync, cloud and an online service).

- **Invoices**: credit notes from the UI (the model and the template support them; the Steuerberater export must include them once they exist), VAT categories and exemption reasons per rate (reverse charge and other VAT-free cases), discounts and surcharges, other payment means (direct debit, card), tax representative, payee, attachments (BG-24), the EAS scheme list for electronic addresses (the form always uses e-mail), a register number for BT-30, customer-specific payment terms, dunning, proper Abschlags- and Schlussrechnungen (deducting earlier partial invoices, § 14 (5) UStG).
- **Offers**: status accepted or declined, a chain of revisions ("ersetzt AN-2026-003"), billed versus offered amount, a counter for a future period, other kinds on the same sequences (Auftragsbestätigung, Lieferschein, Gutschrift as its own sequence); once a third kind exists the table and types could be called `documents`.
- **Contexts**: a kind per context (company, Verein, private person) with its own defaults, moving a draft to another context (it re-applies seller, layout and numbers), an archive view for contexts and invoices, context colours in the invoice list.
- **Output**: `Per E-Mail…`, `Drucken`, "Im Finder zeigen"; re-verifying the kept files against their hashes in a maintenance view; scheduled or automatic backups (a reminder when the last one is old), encrypted backups.
- **Layouts and documents**: EPC / GiroCode QR code for EUR transfers (needs a vendored QR package, no network at compile time), exact DIN 5008 window geometry and fold marks, English invoices (the labels live in `render/view.rs`), `subject`, `notices` and `place` as data, a total-only column mode and a no-VAT totals mode, a second built-in design.
- **Units**: plural or quantity-dependent labels, a default quantity or price per unit, export and import of the list.
- **UI**: sort options of the invoice list, use counts or favourites and several lines for saved positions, a readable maximum width for single-line inputs on very wide windows, zoom and keyboard paging in the preview (it is the template, not the final file, so it cannot show an export failure), frontend tests (flows were checked by hand in the browser mock), rule texts that read oddly (e.g. `BR-CO-26` with only a tax number).
- **Validation and safety**: bump Mustang when a release with veraPDF 1.30.3 exists (`scripts/sidecars.json`), a test that fails if the pipeline ever modifies the PDF after `combine`, checking that Mustang makes no network calls (its input is only our own files), German texts for Mustang's report messages.
- **Release**: the updater end to end (install an older dmg, see the update appear, install, relaunch; the manifest points at the release-asset URLs `tauri-action` writes), signing and notarization including the nested JRE binaries. The Liquid Glass icon: Tauri's bundler compiles a `.icon` listed under `bundle.icon` with `actool` (Xcode 26+), which failed on every `.icon` with the Xcode 27 beta; retry with a release Xcode and add `../src/assets/icon-default.icon` to `bundle.icon`. The changelog only lists `feat`, `fix`, `perf` and `revert` commits (angular preset).
- **By hand in the real app** (`bun run dev`): the whole flow with the real backend (settings, customer, invoice, create, export as PDF, open the PDF and its embedded XML in a ZUGFeRD viewer), the macOS title bar overlay next to the traffic lights, dark and light mode, an invoice with a logo, the updater button.

## Release

Pushing to `main` runs `check`, then bumps the version (conventional commits, `release-it`), builds the app
on macOS into a draft release, commits the bump, publishes the release and writes `latest.json` for the
in-app updater to the `gh-e-rng-latest` branch. Needs the repo secret `TAURI_SIGNING_PRIVATE_KEY`.
The app checks on start and from the menu (`updater.component.ts`): its button first only loads the update while
the user goes on working, then reads `Jetzt 0.7.0 installieren`, so the restart happens when the user says so
(after asking about unsaved changes). The loaded package lives in memory: a restart forgets it and the check on
start offers the newest again, a check that finds a newer version drops it and offers that one. The browser mock
offers one with `?update` (`?update=0.8.0` for that version).
Signed ad hoc (`bundle.macOS.signingIdentity` is `-`), not notarized: no Apple Developer ID. A downloaded copy shows
Gatekeeper's "cannot verify" dialog, and System Settings → Privacy & Security offers "Open Anyway". A bundle whose
signature does not verify would say "damaged" instead, with no way around it: check a build with
`codesign --verify --deep --strict E-RNG.app`. Mind that running a release build uses the real data folder, only
`tauri dev` has its own.
